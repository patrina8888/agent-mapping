# Agent Mapping v2 — Headcount-Weighted Companion View

**HTC internal · 2026-09-03 · companion to the dollar-weighted labor map (frozen taxonomy G2)**

## Why this view exists (the one-paragraph thesis)

The dollar map ranks tracks by **displaceable/captured wage bill = headcount × comp-per-head**. These four families are the map's **low comp-per-head** pools — frontline, blue-collar, public, informal, disproportionately global-South, gig/tipped/minimum-wage/unpaid. So their share of the $60T pool — and especially of the **coupled vendor-realized line (capture-rate × displaced-labor)** — is small *relative to the number of humans whose jobs are actually exposed*. Three compounding reasons the dollar view buries them:

1. **Low comp-per-head.** A cashier, rider, or care worker earns a fraction of a lawyer or senior SWE. The same job-at-risk count maps to a far smaller wage bill, so headcount-huge families rank low on a dollar axis.
2. **Advanced-economy comp weighting** (P0: advanced comp 5–15× developing for the same job). The dollar map is advanced-economy-centric; the *bodies* in W9/W10/W14 sit largely in the developing world and are nearly erased by dollar weighting.
3. **Coupling compression at today's <1% capture.** For coupled physical/frontline tracks, dollar-TAM = capture × displaced, and realized capture is <1%. So the *current* dollar-realized line is almost invisible even where the eventual headcount displacement is the largest single event in the map.

**All 5-yr ranges are realized/deployed displacement — the technically-exposed share, then heavily discounted for the pilot-to-production chasm** (MIT 95% zero-P&L, S&P 42% abandonment, <15% to production) and each track's hard gate. "Jobs at risk" = roles with material probability of elimination or non-replacement (layoff *or* attrition/hiring-freeze) within 5 years, **not** task-automation ceilings. Coupling rule obeyed throughout: displacement is a headcount count, never SW-ARR summed onto labor.

> **De-dup caveat (mirrors the framework's "never sum" discipline):** Uber's ~10M "drivers and couriers" straddles W9.1 (rideshare) and W9.3 (couriers); China's ~11–12M riders are W9.3; the ~200–400M global gig pool spans W9.1/W9.3/W10.3. Do **not** naively add track headcounts into a family total without netting the gig overlap.

## W9 — Transportation & Mobility

| Track | Global headcount | 5-yr jobs-at-risk (headcount) | Gating factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W9.1** Rideshare, Taxi & Robotaxi | **~40–55M** rideshare + taxi drivers (Uber ~10M drivers+couriers; DiDi ~13M active China / ~19M annual; + Grab/Bolt/Ola/Lyft + traditional taxi) | **~1–4M** (~2–8%) — robotaxi real but metro-confined (Waymo, Baidu Apollo Go, Tesla, Zoox); displacement concentrates in a handful of US/China cities | **R=5** DOT/safety/insurance + per-city permitting + robot capex; capture <1% |
| **W9.2** Long-haul & Regional Trucking | **~40–70M** goods-vehicle drivers (China ~30M all-truck [2014]; US 3.54M; EU ~3M+; India large). Heavy long-haul subset ~25–35M | **~1–3M** — driverless corridors live (Aurora Dallas–Houston 2025, Kodiak, Waabi) but Sun-Belt/highway-only; **offset by a 2.9–3.6M driver shortage** that automation fills before it displaces | **R=5** safety/insurance/regulation **+ chronic driver shortage masks displacement** (fills gap, blunts backlash) |
| **W9.3** Last-mile Delivery & Courier | **~20–40M** couriers/riders (China food delivery ~11–12M; Amazon DSP; DoorDash/Uber Eats/Instacart/postal parcel) — subset of the 200–400M gig pool | **~0.5–2M** — sidewalk bots (Starship), drones (Zipline/Wing), AV vans early; physical last-mile is the hardest to automate; **e-commerce demand is adding riders faster than automation removes them** (near-term net expansion) | **Hardware/dexterity too-early** + R=4; demand growth outruns automation |

**Why dollar-weighting understates W9:** professional driving is sub-median, gig/informal, and global-South-heavy — a ~$1.1–2.0T-in-headcount-terms workforce (~100M+ bodies after de-dup) that the dollar map carries at only $1.1–2.0T of *advanced-economy-weighted* wage bill and a near-zero coupled vendor line (<1% capture). The families that will generate the loudest displacement politics (driver unions, robotaxi city fights) are ranked as dollar footnotes.

| Track | Global headcount | 5-yr jobs-at-risk (headcount) | Gating factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W10.1** Retail Floor & Cashiering | **~200–300M** retail frontline (global wholesale+retail trade ~450M per ILO; retail-only ~300–350M; US retail 16.3M). **Cashiers specifically ~100M+** (US ~3.3M) | **~15–40M** — the map's **single largest displacement event by bodies**; WEF Future of Jobs 2025 ranks cashiers/checkout clerks the **#1 absolute-decline occupation**; self-checkout + vision-checkout already deployed. Sales-floor/stock more durable | Capital intensity + CX choice; checkout automation is the **live exception** to "too-early"; V=3 |
| **W10.2** Hospitality & Hotel Operations | **~20–60M** direct hotel/accommodation ops (within WTTC's **366M** total travel & tourism jobs, 2025) | **~2–6M** — front-desk/check-in kiosks, concierge/booking chatbots hit front-office; **housekeeping/physical service durable** (too-early, dexterous) | **Too-early** physical presence + guest-experience preference for humans in premium tiers |
| **W10.3** Food-Service Front-of-House & Ordering | **~60–100M** food-service/waitstaff/QSR counter (US food & beverage serving ~5.1M) | **~5–15M** — kiosk/app/QR ordering + **voice-AI drive-thru live at QSRs** (∩ W3.6) compress counter/ordering roles; table service durable | **Presence-gated** + franchise economics; ordering-layer is the live wedge |

**Why dollar-weighting understates W10:** retail cashiering is the extreme case. Cashiers are near-minimum-wage, so the world's **largest single displaced occupation by bodies** appears as a modest wage bill ($0.8–1.2T) and a near-zero coupled vendor TAM. Dollar-ranked, frontline retail/food looks minor; headcount-ranked, W10.1+W10.3 alone plausibly carry **20–55M** of the WEF's 92M-by-2030 displaced-jobs figure — a majority-share of *human* displacement sitting at a minority-share of *dollar* TAM.

## W11 — Public-Sector Delivery

| Track | Global headcount | 5-yr jobs-at-risk (headcount) | Gating factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W11.1** Benefits/Case Processing & Citizen Service | **~10–30M** addressable public-admin clerical/case subset (public sector = **~11% of world employment ≈ ~375M** incl. education/health/security; core admin is a slice) | **~1–4M** — high automatability (V=4, coupled/Compression) but realized displacement is **attrition/non-replacement, not layoffs** | **R=5** procurement + political + civil-service protection |
| **W11.2** Public Education Delivery | **~85M** K–12 teachers (UNESCO; ~94–100M incl. tertiary) — **largest single headcount pool in W11** | **<1–2M net** — **Additive/Expansion**: AI tutoring augments; effect is *easing the 44M-teacher shortfall*, not cutting jobs | **R=5 blocked** — political + in-person schooling + credentialing |
| **W11.3** Public-Safety Records & Permitting | **~3–10M** records/permitting/licensing clerks & dispatchers | **~0.5–2M** — coupled/Compression via attrition; incumbent-gov captured (Palantir/ServiceNow-gov) | **R=5** procurement + political (blocked) |

**Why dollar-weighting understates W11:** here the dollar TAM is actually *large* ($1.5–2.5T for education alone — because there are ~85–100M teachers) yet its **displacement** contribution is near-zero (Additive/Expansion, blocked). The understatement is different in kind: a pure dollar-TAM lens cannot price the **social/political weight** of who delivers citizen services and of teacher employment as social policy — those aren't dollar quantities. W11.2 is the honest counter-case where both views agree it's Expansion, not displacement; W11.1/W11.3 are capability-ready but gate-throttled, so headcount-at-risk stays modest despite high automatability.

## W14 — Consumer/Household

| Track | Global headcount | 5-yr jobs-at-risk (headcount) | Gating factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W14.1** Consumer/Household Personal Assistance | **75.6M paid domestic workers** (ILO; possibly **up to 100M** informal) **+ ~$11T latent unpaid household/care economy** (billions of people, ~76% women) | **~<0.5–1M paid-worker displacement** — physical/dexterous/in-home/relational (L=5); **the action is [L] demand-CREATION/Expansion** (consumer AI doing what people would never have paid a human for), not displacement of paid domestics | **Physical dexterity + relational trust + informality/unpaid**; the work is mostly off-payroll |

**Why dollar-weighting understates W14:** the sharpest case in the whole framework. Only the **$50–100B paid slice** enters the dollar map. The **75.6M+ paid domestic workers** and the **~$11T unpaid/latent care economy** (the single largest number anywhere in the taxonomy, and billions of human hours) are almost entirely off the dollar ledger. A dollar-ranked "total agent TAM" reduces the planet's largest human-labor pool to a rounding error — and misses that this is where consumer-AI's *largest realized revenue* actually lands, as expansion of a latent pool, not compression of a paid one.

## Synthesis — what the headcount axis changes

- **Scale re-ranking.** These four families touch on the order of **~600–800M workers** (retail frontline ~200–300M; food service ~60–100M; hospitality tens of millions; transport ~100M+ net of gig overlap; public-sector delivery incl. ~85–100M teachers; ~76–100M domestic) — yet they are a **minority of the dollar-weighted $60T pool** and carry a **near-zero coupled vendor line** at today's <1% capture. Dollar-ranking silently biases "total agent TAM" against blue-collar/public/consumer labor.
- **Where the human displacement actually concentrates.** Realistic 5-yr at-risk headcount is dominated by **retail cashiering (W10.1, ~15–40M)** and **food-service ordering (W10.3, ~5–15M)** — exactly the WEF #1/#2 absolute-decline occupations — plus modest, gate-throttled transport and public-clerical erosion. This is a large share of WEF's **92M jobs displaced by 2030**, sitting where the dollar map shows almost nothing.
- **Two distinct understatement mechanisms.** (a) *Comp-erasure* (W9, W10, W14): huge bodies × low pay + <1% capture → invisible in dollars. (b) *Non-dollar weight* (W11): the wage bill is counted, but civic/social significance and the political economy of displacement are not priceable on a dollar axis.
- **The gates, not the models, set the pace.** Every 5-yr range is gate-limited — R=5 (mobility, public sector), hardware/dexterity clock (last-mile, housekeeping, domestic), or capital/CX choice (retail, food) — and further discounted for the pilot-to-production chasm. Capability is ahead of every one of these numbers; watch the **gate's movement** (robotaxi city permits, driverless-freight insurance, self-checkout capex, public procurement) as the falsifiable resolver, per HTC's conviction-with-triggers style.
- **Investment read (unchanged from frozen picks):** headcount weight does *not* reopen seed doors here — W9/W10/W14 are robotics-timeline or incumbent/capex plays, not seed software. The companion view's job is **honesty in the denominator**, so the published "total agent TAM" isn't read as a claim that blue-collar/public/consumer labor is unaffected. It's the most-exposed *humans*, the least-exposed *dollars*.

**Confidence:** headcounts are Tier-1-anchored where a clean global figure exists (teachers UNESCO ~85M; domestic ILO 75.6M; Uber 10-K 10M; public sector 11% ILO; retail sector ~450M ILO; tourism WTTC 366M). Truck-driver, taxi+rideshare, last-mile, hotel-ops-direct, and public-admin-clerical globals are **triangulated bands (soft)** — no single authority publishes them; ranges reflect that. All 5-yr at-risk ranges are analyst-judgment overlays on those headcounts under the pilot-to-production discount, not measured series.
